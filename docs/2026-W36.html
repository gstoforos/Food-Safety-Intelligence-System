§ 01

Intelligence Analysis

This week produced 80 food-safety hazard recall incidents across the AFTS monitoring network, with 59 classified as Tier-1 and 4 outbreak-linked notices (recall expansions and agency updates) tied to investigations already open before this window. Listeria monocytogenes dominated the surveillance window, accounting for 30 of 80 incidents (38%). Although total activity may vary week to week, the high Tier-1 share indicates that serious microbiological and chemical hazards continue to drive regulatory action.

**Outbreak watch:** Four notices were linked to ongoing outbreak investigations this week: Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC) and Salmonella linked to Robust Radish Mix, 5 oz cups (recall expansion; investigation open since 22 August 2026; no illness associated with this product to date); E. coli O145 (STEC) linked to Organic Triple Berry Blend (recall expansion; investigation open since 6 July 2026; no illness associated with this product to date); Hepatitis A virus linked to frozen concha negra (agency update; investigation open since 5 June 2026); and Salmonella Enteritidis linked to shell eggs (agency update; investigation opened 22 July 2026, now closed). None of these represents a new illness cluster: each notice is a recall expansion or an agency update on an investigation already open, and case counts belong to the investigations, not to the recalled lots. Although Listeria monocytogenes remained the leading incident driver, these investigations should remain under active monitoring. Distribution scope is as stated by the notifying authority; case geography is not distribution geography.

Listeria monocytogenes at this concentration is found mainly in ready-to-eat categories (deli, dairy, and cooked-meat lines). The notices do not establish the contamination route: post-process recontamination from environmental harbourage, sanitation drift, raw-material entry, and inadequate lethality are all hypotheses that only product and facility evidence can distinguish. The controls an operator should re-verify against a signal like this are the environmental monitoring programme and corrective-action triggers (21 CFR 117 in the United States; Regulation (EC) 2073/2005 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/2895 in the EU) together with the validated lethality step for the product class—both, not one or the other. The frameworks cited above are United States instruments, provided here as the most fully specified statement of the control. The majority of this week's incidents fall outside that jurisdiction, where equivalent duties sit in Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 (hygiene and HACCP) and Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 (microbiological criteria), with national competent-authority oversight.

Notices this week were published by RappelConso, FSA, FDA, RASFF, BLV, and other national authorities. A recall count records published notices, not inspection effort; they arise from official controls, company own-checks, supplier information, and precautionary withdrawals alike. AFTS recommends that food manufacturers use this briefing as a prompt to re-verify the single highest-leverage control for their commodity this week and to confirm that documentation packages are ready for rapid regulatory response.

Process Authority Note: **[[PA-NOTE]]** This window contains 35 Listeria monocytogenes incidents, with Fromagerie de Burdignes (Fromagerie les 4 Fermes) (France), U Saveurs (France), and 3 Provinces (France) among those cited. Ready-to-eat (RTE) manufacturers—particularly those producing deli meats, soft cheese and dairy (including raw-milk cheese), charcuterie and cured meats, smoked and cured seafood, cooked meats, refrigerated prepared salads, and cut produce—should review their environmental monitoring programme (EMP) and corrective-action triggers under Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/2895 (applicable from 1 July 2026): for RTE foods able to support Listeria monocytogenes growth, other than those intended for infants or special medical purposes, the criterion applies to products placed on the market throughout their shelf life; the limit is 100 CFU/g only where the operator can demonstrate to the competent authority that the product will not exceed it during shelf life, and otherwise not detected in 25 g. Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 HACCP requirements apply, and operators must notify and cooperate with the national competent authority, which is the body that issues any RASFF notification. Parallel frameworks apply under retained EU Regulation 2073/2005, UK Food Hygiene Regulations, and FSA listeria-in-RTE control guidance; CFIA Policy on Control of Listeria monocytogenes in Ready-to-Eat Foods and the SFCR Preventive Control Plan; FSANZ Food Standards Code Standard 1.6.1 (microbiological limits) and industry Listeria management guidelines. § 05

Methodology & Sources

Process authority. Analytical frameworks, severity rubrics, pathogen classification, and the engineering interpretation of each recall are developed by the AFTS process-authority practice, drawing on in-house expertise in food process engineering, thermal processing, and regulatory compliance. Every view is grounded in validated process engineering: thermal processing (21 CFR 113/114), pasteurisation (PMO), aseptic and UHT, hold-tube and F-value lethality, and HACCP. This is what the AFTS platform brings that pure data feeds do not — interpretation under engineering authority.

Severity tiers. Tier 1 — pathogens and toxins that cause severe or life-threatening illness at the doses found in food (Listeria monocytogenes, Salmonella, STEC, Clostridium botulinum, cereulide, Cronobacter, hepatitis A, Vibrio vulnificus), always, whatever the regulator's own class. Tier 2 — hazards that cause acute but usually self-limiting illness or chronic-exposure harm (Campylobacter, Bacillus cereus, generic Vibrio, mycotoxins, heavy metals, pesticide residues, undeclared pharmacological ingredients). Tier 3 — hazards whose harm is injury or contamination rather than infection or toxicity (foreign bodies in food for the general population, pests), raised to Tier 1 only in products for infants and other vulnerable groups. A foreign body of unspecified material is Tier 3 even when the notice cites an injury risk; that risk is why the recall exists, not a reason to rank it beside a pathogen. Where a notice names two hazards the row carries both and the incident is counted once.

Data & AI pipeline. The system aggregates regulatory recall notices from 66 primary sources across 60+ countries (FDA, USDA FSIS, RASFF, FSA, FSANZ, CFIA, RappelConso, BVL, AESAN, EFET, and national authorities) and processes each record through Gemini (extraction), OpenAI GPT (normalisation), and Claude (Tier-1 validation). Records are de-duplicated and harmonised into the cumulative dataset.

This briefing. Statistical analysis filters the cumulative dataset to the reporting week (28 Aug – 6 Sep 2026). AI-generated narrative is produced against AFTS process-authority prompts and edited for publication. Figures and pathogen names are preserved verbatim from source data.
