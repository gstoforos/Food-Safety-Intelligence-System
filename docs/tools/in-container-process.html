Method

The equation

Transient conduction in a finite cylinder, axisymmetric in r and z:

ρcp ∂T/∂t = (1/r) ∂/∂r ( k r ∂T/∂r ) + ∂/∂z ( k ∂T/∂z )

solved by Galerkin finite elements — bilinear quadrilaterals on a structured (r, z) mesh, 2×2 Gauss quadrature, and the axisymmetric volume weight 2πr. The symmetry axis needs no condition of its own: that weight vanishes there. The wall and both ends carry a convective condition, q = h(Tmedium − Tsurface); a condensing-steam retort is modelled as an isothermal surface through a scaled penalty on h.

Time integration is the generalised θ-method. θ = 1 is backward Euler: first order, unconditionally stable and monotone in time, which is what a come-up meeting a hold needs. The system matrix is constant, so it is Cholesky-factorised once in banded storage and back-substituted per step.

Lethality

The lethal rate L = 10(T−Tref)/z is integrated by trapezoid at every node, and the mass-average F is the volume-weighted mean of those nodal F-values. It is not F of the mass-average temperature: L is convex in T, so those are different numbers, and the second one is the flattering one.

The cold spot is reported as the node of least accumulated lethality — found, not assumed. For pure conduction in a cylinder heated on every face it lands on the geometric centre, and the schedule table says whether it did.

Heat penetration

fh and jh are read off the simulated centre curve by least squares over the part of the constant-temperature hold where the unaccomplished temperature difference has fallen between 0.30 and 0.02 of its initial value — after the higher modes have died, before the curve flattens into rounding. Fit from 0.5 and the curve is still approaching its asymptote from below, which flattens the slope and inflates fh by around 3 % on a normal can.

jh is referenced to the start of the hold, so it is the textbook 2.04 when the come-up is instantaneous and drifts below it when it is not. Ball's 0.42 × come-up correction is deliberately not applied: this reports what the model did, not what Ball's method would have assumed. The closed form it is checked against is fh = ln(10)/λ1, with λ1 = α((2.4048/R)² + (π/H)²).

What is checked

The solver behind this page is verified against the closed-form finite-cylinder product solution — the Bessel series in r times the cosine series in z — against Ball's fh and jh for the same geometry, and against the discrete invariants of its own assembly: the capacity matrix must integrate to ρcpV and the conduction matrix must annihilate a uniform field, both to machine precision. Every run on this page also re-solves on a doubled mesh at half the time step and reports the difference, because a converged-looking answer on one mesh is not a converged answer.

What this model is not

It is conduction only: no convection inside the pack, no agitation, no rotation, no headspace, no boiling, no phase change, no shrinkage, and uniform properties that do not vary with temperature. It assumes the medium temperature is the same everywhere outside the container, which a temperature-distribution study is what establishes. A convection-heating or broken-heating product is outside this model and its answer here will be optimistic.

Thermophysical properties are the input that decides the answer. The presets are representative handbook values and nothing more; a 10 % error in diffusivity is a 10 % error in fh, which at the end of a long conduction cook is a large error in F.

Regulatory

Across jurisdictions the regulatory architecture differs but the engineering principle converges: a shelf-stable low-acid or commercially sterile product requires a scientifically established and validated process, executed under documented GMP oversight.

United States

21 CFR 113 — thermally processed low-acid foods packaged in hermetically sealed containers. 21 CFR 114 — acidified foods. 21 CFR 108 — emergency permit control, and the registration and process-filing obligations. 9 CFR 431 for meat and poultry. The scheduled process must be established by a qualified process authority, and a deviation must be evaluated by one before release.

European Union

Regulation 852/2004 imposes general hygiene and HACCP obligations; Regulation 2073/2005 fixes microbiological criteria. Neither is a direct equivalent of US LACF filing, but both presuppose process control validated against a documented hazard analysis, with national low-acid rules layered on top in some Member States.

Elsewhere

Canada's SFCR, the UK's FSA / FSS, FSANZ in Australia and New Zealand and Japan's MHLW each impose comparable preventive-control obligations to the same end.

This page is not a scheduled process. It is a model, run on the numbers typed into it. A scheduled process is established by a qualified process authority on measured product data, against a temperature-distribution study of the retort it will run in, and it is signed. Nothing computed here substitutes for that, and no number on this page should be filed, quoted to a regulator or run in a plant on its own authority.
