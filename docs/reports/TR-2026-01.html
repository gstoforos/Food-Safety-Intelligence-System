1What this is, and what it is not

This is a retrospective aberration detector over regulatory recall notices. It answers one question: is a given stratum — a pathogen, a country, a hazard tier, or a combination — running above its own recent baseline this week?

It does not forecast. The corpus has no denominator: no production volumes, no consumption data, no negative examples. Nothing here supports a statement of the form “product X is likely to be recalled”, and no output string in the module makes one. A rise in notices is first a fact about publishers and only second a fact about food.

The corpus covers 34 complete weeks, 29 Dec 2025 to 17 Aug 2026, holding 1,506 notices. The trailing partial week is dropped at load: a week still being populated always looks like a decline, and including it would both suppress genuine signals and poison every baseline it later enters.

2Method

2.1 Detector

CDC EARS C1/C2/C3 (Hutwagner et al.), chosen over Farrington and Noufaily because those need three to five years of history for their seasonal terms and this corpus holds 34 weeks. The stratum contract is designed to survive a migration to Farrington without changing callers, once the history exists.

  • C1 — baseline is the 7 weeks immediately preceding the test week.
  • C2 — same width, offset by a 2-week guard band, so a slow-onset event cannot quietly raise the baseline it is being measured against.
  • C3 — the sum of the last 3 C2 excesses, which catches sustained low-grade drift that no single week would trip.

2.2 Two channels, and why the share channel is the one that alarms

RappelConso (FR) and RASFF (EU) together supply 76% of all records. When one of them clears a backlog, every raw count in the corpus rises at once, and a count-only detector alarms on all of them simultaneously — a publisher event wearing the costume of a food-safety event.

So two channels are computed. The count channel compares the raw weekly count against its baseline mean and standard deviation; it is diagnostic. The share channel compares the stratum's proportion of that week's whole corpus against the pooled baseline share, by exact binomial test; shares are invariant to publisher volume, and this is the channel that alarms. A count signal with no matching share signal is reported as a probable publication artefact.

Effect follows the channel

Until 27 August 2026 the effect column carried the share ratio for every signal, including count-only ones. On the signals in this report the two ratios disagree on 61 of 61. One disagreement was disqualifying rather than cosmetic: a count-only Listeria signal for France carried a share ratio of 0.85 — below one — telling the reader the stratum fell in the same row that said it alarmed. The effect column now follows the channel. Both ratios are retained on every record, so no previously published figure becomes unrecoverable.

2.3 Sparsity ladder

Most pathogen × country cells hold under one record a week. Testing them directly turns 1→3 into a “threefold jump”. Each stratum is therefore tested only at the finest level whose baseline mean clears MIN_BASELINE_MEAN and whose observed count clears MIN_ABSOLUTE_COUNT; otherwise it rolls up, country → region → global.

2.4 Multiplicity

434 strata are evaluated per run. At α = 0.01 that yields several false positives every week, which trains a reader to ignore the feed — the failure mode that matters most for an operational product. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.10 is applied across all strata within a run, and the output is capped at 12 signals ranked by effect.

A source's first weeks of records are a fact about the scraper, not about the food supply, and a baseline drawn from them will read any subsequent normal week as an increase. 3.1 Maturity dates are frozen once determined

Defect found and fixed, 27 August 2026

Maturity was originally computed against the most recent eight weeks — a moving reference. The consequence was not drift but oscillation: one source's maturity moved five times across roughly eleven weeks, and another alternated between two dates eight weeks apart. Because the analytical window opens 9 weeks after the latest maturity among continuous sources, a single source flipping could have moved the window forward by eight weeks and silently excluded six of the seven signal weeks in this report.

Maturity is now frozen at first determination and carries its basis. A determination made on an unripe window is marked provisional and is not frozen. --verify-stability exits non-zero if a rebuild would move any cutoff, so this cannot regress unobserved.

3.2 The analytical window

The latest maturity among continuously collected sources is 6 Apr 2026. A test week is only defensible once its entire baseline lies after that date, which is 7 baseline weeks plus 2 guard weeks later. The window therefore opens 8 Jun 2026.

Two things about that date are worth stating plainly. It is derived by code, not asserted: signal_detector.coverage_window_start() returns it from the register. And until 27 August 2026 the detector contained no reference to the coverage register at all — the window in the first draft of this report had been applied by hand. The gate is now a property of the code. It is deliberately additive: signals outside the window are flagged, not dropped, unless a caller asks for enforcement, because silently changing what a detector reports is a worse cure than the disease.

5Results: walk-forward replay

Every week is scored using only weeks strictly before it. This is not cross-validation — random folds on surveillance time series leak the future into the baseline and produce beautiful, meaningless numbers. 22 weeks were scored; the first 12 are consumed by the baseline and guard band and cannot be scored at all.

There is no labelled anomaly set, so these tables are not an accuracy measurement. They are the alarm ledger: each row is reviewed by hand and marked genuine or artefact, and that log becomes the calibration record.

5.1 How a row is admitted — the two channels differ

This matters for reading the last column, and it is not symmetric. A share row is admitted by an exact binomial test at α = 0.01 and must then survive Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10 across every stratum tested that week. A count-only row is admitted by the EARS statistic alone — C1 or C2 at 3.0, or C3 at 2.0, with the observed count at least 20% above baseline — and bypasses FDR by design, because it is not being offered as a discovery. It is being logged as a probable publisher artefact, and suppressing those by multiplicity control would hide exactly the rows an operator needs to see in order to recognise a backlog dump.

Do not read the two channels as one scale

A count-only row carries a binomial p-value in the underlying record, and that number is frequently near 1.0 — which is the point: the count rose while the share did not, so the share test correctly declines to alarm. Reporting that p-value beside a count-only row would invite the reader to conclude the row is meaningless, when what it actually documents is a publication event. The column below therefore shows each row's own admission criterion, never the other channel's.

6Limitations

No denominator. Notice counts are not incidence. A country that publishes diligently will look worse than one that does not, and this detector cannot distinguish the two. Every stratum is compared only against itself.

Short history. 34 weeks supports EARS and nothing seasonal. Any pattern with an annual period is invisible, and a genuine seasonal rise will be read as an aberration until at least three years of history exists.

Publisher events dominate the count channel. Mitigated by the share channel, not eliminated. A backlog clearance concentrated in one hazard class will still move a share.

Outbreaks that never generate a notice are invisible. Some outbreaks surface through clinical surveillance rather than product testing, and the implicated commodity is identified only after case interviews converge. A recent multi-state Cyclospora cluster is the clean example: FSIS observes the regulatory record, and where no notice is issued until an investigation concludes, there is nothing in the corpus for the detector to see. This is a structural limit of recall-derived signal, not a tuning failure, and no amount of threshold work addresses it.

Coverage inference is not ground truth. Onset and maturity are inferred from publication behaviour because no regulator publishes a machine-readable statement of when its own feed became complete. §4 shows how much the inference depends on constants that were chosen by judgement.
